## 1. Story decisions and evidence

### Episode purpose

Introduce Atan through the unusual crown-refusal account, then show why his public career matters: he was a senior minister involved in resistance and recovery during an invasion. The opening question is answered within this Reel. Its final question leads naturally to preparation for renewed conflict, without claiming that the first invasion ended in an uncomplicated Ahom victory.

**Dramatic progression:** the empty seat creates a question → an attributed refusal identifies the protagonist → the 1662 invasion establishes stakes → Atan's existing office and contribution supply agency → the 1663 treaty separates resistance from recovery → the closing question gives recovery a concrete purpose.

**Performance interpretation:** Atan is played as attentive and deliberate. This is a dramatic direction, not evidence of his private thoughts or personality in a particular meeting. There is no invented character dialogue, refusal speech, coronation ceremony, private childhood or supernatural power.

## 2. Narration and timed scene breakdown

### Final planning script — conversational Hinglish

Read the numerals aloud as **“solah sau baasath”** and **“solah sau tirsath”**. These ordinary Hindi number readings are distinct from the Assamese name-pronunciation check described below.

> Raja banne ka mauka mile, toh kaun mana karega?
>
> Itihaskar Bhuyan likhte hain—Atan Burhagohain ne do baar taaj thukraya.
>
> Lekin pehle chalte hain... 1662 ke Assam mein.
>
> Mughal senapati Mir Jumla ne rajdhani Garhgaon par kabza kar liya.
>
> Raja ko rajdhani chhodkar hatna pada.
>
> Atan January mein hi Burhagohain bane the—Ahom rajya ke ek pramukh mantri.
>
> Unhone Mughal sena ke khilaaf lad rahe logon ko ekjut kiya.
>
> 1663 mein sandhi hui. Phir rajya ko sambhalne mein bhi Atan ki aham bhoomika rahi.
>
> Aage woh Lachit Borphukan ke saath bhi ladenge. Lekin usse pehle...
>
> Is hamle ke baad, Assam dobara ladne layak kaise banega?

**Narrator direction:** one warm, clear adult narrator; curious opening, grounded urgency through invasion, steadier delivery through recovery. “Do baar” receives a small pause, not a shouted punchline. The treaty line is sober. Finish the final question before the last quiet hold. This episode has voiceover only; Atan's mouth stays at rest.

**Timing honesty:** 105 whitespace/punctuation-tokenised Roman-script words; the two written years expand into several spoken words. This is not a measured speaking duration. Allow approximately 45–52 seconds of actual speech inside the 60-second edit, with deliberate pauses. Never stretch every line to fill its entire scene. Record a complete scratch read before caption alignment; preserve scene IDs if internal boundaries need modest adjustment.
